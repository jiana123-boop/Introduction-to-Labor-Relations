SECTION 01

Meaning & Objectives

Understanding labor relations and its aims

01

Richard A. Lester — Labor Relations

PDF p. 2
  • Labor relations involve attempts to find workable solutions between conflicting objectives and values.
  • Between incentives and economic security; discipline and industrial democracy.
  • Between authority and freedom; bargaining and operation.

Dale Yoder — Labor Relations

PDF p. 2
  • Labor relations is the relationship of management and employees, or among employees or their organizations.

Objectives of Labor Relations

PDF p. 3–4
  • Safeguard the interests of labor through the highest level of mutual understanding.
  • Avoid industrial conflict and develop harmonious relations between workers and management.
  • Increase organizational productivity by reducing absenteeism and turnover.
  • Establish and maintain industrial democracy through employee participation and equal benefits.
  • Improve the image, growth, and development of industries.
  • Maintain industrial peace and stability through collective bargaining.
  • Eliminate problems with industrial relations.

SECTION 03

Labor Code — Article 219

Definitions in Presidential Decree No. 442 of 1974

03

Employer

PDF p. 6
  • Includes any person acting in the interest of an employer, directly or indirectly.
  • Does not include a labor organization or any of its officers or agents, except when acting as an employer.

Employee

PDF p. 6
  • Includes any person in the employ of an employer; not limited to employees of a particular employer unless the Code explicitly states otherwise.
  • Includes individuals whose work has ceased because of a current labor dispute or unfair labor practice, if they have not obtained other substantially equivalent and regular employment.

Managerial Employee

PDF p. 7
  • Has the power to establish and execute management policies and/or hire, transfer, suspend, lay off, recall, discharge, assign, or discipline employees.

Supervisory Employee

PDF p. 7
  • In the employer’s interest, effectively recommends managerial actions using independent judgment, rather than solely routine or clerical authority.

Commission

PDF p. 8
  • National Labor Relations Commission or any of its divisions.

Bureau

PDF p. 8
  • Bureau of Labor Relations and/or Labor Relations Divisions in Department of Labor regional offices, established under Presidential Decree No. 1.

Board

PDF p. 8
  • National Conciliation and Mediation Board established under Executive Order No. 126.
SECTION 04

Labor Organizations & Disputes

Further definitions under Article 219

04

Labor Organization

PDF p. 8
  • Any union or association of employees existing wholly or in part for collective bargaining, or dealing with employers about terms and conditions of employment.

Legitimate Labor Organization

PDF p. 9
  • A labor organization, including its branches, duly registered with the Department of Labor and Employment.

Company Union

PDF p. 9
  • Any labor organization whose formation, function, or administration was assisted by an act defined in the Code as an unfair labor practice.

Bargaining Representative

PDF p. 9
  • A legitimate labor organization or any officer or agent of it, whether or not that person is employed by the employer.

Unfair Labor Practice

PDF p. 9
  • An unfair labor practice as expressly defined by the Code.

Labor Dispute

PDF p. 10
  • A controversy regarding terms and conditions of employment, or the association or representation of people in negotiating, fixing, maintaining, changing, or arranging those terms.
  • A labor dispute may exist regardless of whether the disputants have a proximate employer–employee relationship.

Voluntary Arbitrator

PDF p. 10
  • A person accredited by the Board and designated in the collective bargaining agreement by the parties to act as voluntary arbitrator.
SECTION 05

Industrial Actions

Important terms in labor disputes

05

Strike

PDF p. 11
  • Temporary stoppage of work by the concerted action of employees as a result of an industrial or labor dispute.

Lockout

PDF p. 11
  • Temporary refusal by an employer to furnish work as a result of an industrial or labor dispute.

Strike-Breaker

PDF p. 11
  • A person who obstructs, impedes, or interferes through force, violence, coercion, threats, or intimidation with peaceful picketing affecting work conditions or the exercise of self-organization or collective bargaining.

Strike Area

PDF p. 11
  • Includes the establishment, warehouses, depots, plants or offices, including sites or premises used as runaway shops by picketing strikers.
